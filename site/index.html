Look up from the screen.

Recess is a free, open-source break reminder for Mac and Windows. When it's time to rest your eyes, stretch or get up, it gently dims the screen, and steps back the moment you return.

Download for Mac Take a 20-second break

For macOS 14 or later, or download for Windows.

Small breaks, on a rhythm.

Out of the box there are two: a few seconds every quarter of an hour to unclench and look across the room, and ten minutes each hour to properly get up. Change either, or add your own.

Small details that make it stick.

Fits around your day

  • Start on time, wait until you stop typing, or ask first.
  • Only in work hours, only on weekdays, or timed from your calendar.
  • Holds off during video calls, presentations and fullscreen apps.
  • Time away from the keyboard, and sleep, count as rest.

Keeps you honest

  • Cap how many times a day you can postpone or skip.
  • Lock the buttons for the first few seconds of a break.
  • Optionally block Cmd-Tab and Force Quit during a break.
  • A chart of breaks taken, postponed and skipped.

Yours to shape

  • Break screens with a breathing guide, clocks, eye and desk stretches, or your own photos, text or video.
  • Sounds, spoken reminders, and your own scripts: AppleScript or shell on a Mac, PowerShell on Windows.
  • A countdown in the menu bar or notification area, and global keyboard shortcuts.
  • recess:// links for Shortcuts and automation.

Installing takes a minute.

Recess isn't notarized by Apple yet, so the first time you open it macOS asks you to confirm. You only do this once. It needs macOS 14 Sonoma or later, on Apple Silicon or Intel.

  1. Open Recess.dmg and drag Recess into Applications.
  2. Open Recess. When macOS says it can't verify the app, click Done.
  3. In System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll to Security and click Open Anyway.
  4. Confirm with Touch ID or your password, then click Open.
Rather use Terminal? 
xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Recess.app
open /Applications/Recess.app

Recess for Windows isn't code-signed yet, so Windows may stop it the first time. It installs for your account only, without admin rights, on Windows 10 (version 2004) or 11.

  1. Run Recess-Setup.exe.
  2. If Windows says it protected your PC, click More info, then Run anyway.
  3. Recess opens with a short setup, then lives by the clock in the notification area.
  4. To keep its icon in view, turn Recess on under Settings › Personalization › Taskbar.

Rather not install? Recess-windows.zip is a portable copy: unzip it anywhere and run Recess.exe.

Nothing leaves your computer.

No account, no analytics, and no network requests of its own; on Windows it fetches only the calendars you add. Your settings are plain files on your computer. Recess is MIT licensed, so you can read every line, or build it yourself: 

git clone https://github.com/NeelkanthTandel/recess.git
cd recess && make install                                  # Mac
cd recess\windows; dotnet run --project src/Recess.App -c Release  # Windows

Questions, answered.

Is Recess really free?

Yes. There's no trial, subscription, paid tier or advert, and no account to make. Recess is open source under the MIT License, so anyone can read, change and share the code.

Does it work on Mac and Windows?

Both. On a Mac it needs macOS 14 Sonoma or later, on Apple Silicon or Intel. On Windows it needs Windows 10 (version 2004) or Windows 11, 64-bit, and it also runs on Windows on Arm. The breaks, rules and themes are the same on both.

What's the 20-20-20 rule, and can Recess follow it?

Every 20 minutes, look at something about 20 feet (6 metres) away for 20 seconds. It's a rule of thumb eye doctors suggest for tired eyes at a screen. Recess's micro-break starts at 15 seconds every 15 minutes; set it to 20 seconds every 20 minutes and you have the rule exactly. Recess is a reminder, not medical advice: if your eyes keep hurting, see an eye doctor.

Can I use it as a Pomodoro timer?

For the breaks, yes. Make a 5-minute break every 25 minutes, timed from when the last one ended, and add a longer break every two hours; when two are due together, the bigger one wins. Recess times your breaks rather than your tasks, so there's no to-do list.

Will it remind me to stretch, stand up or sit up straight?

It starts with a ten-minute break every hour to get up and move. Break screens can walk you through eye and desk stretches or a breathing guide, or show your own words, like a reminder to check your posture. It never uses your camera; it only reminds you.

Does it work with more than one screen?

Yes. The break appears on your main screen, and you choose whether the others show it too, simply dim, or stay as they are.

Will it interrupt a video call or a presentation?

Not if you don't want it to. Breaks can wait while a fullscreen app or a given app is open, during a calendar event, or, on Windows, while your microphone or camera is in use. A break can also wait until you stop typing, or ask first.

Why does my computer warn me when I open it?

Recess isn't notarized by Apple or code-signed for Windows yet, so each asks you to confirm once, the first time. The code is public, each release lists the SHA-256 checksum of every download, and the app makes no network requests of its own.

How is it different from other break reminders?

Plenty of good ones exist. Recess is for people who want one that costs nothing, works the same on a Mac and a Windows PC, needs no account, keeps everything on the computer, and can be gentle or strict: you can cap postpones and skips, or lock the computer into the break.

Who makes Recess?

Neelkanth Tandel and the people who contribute on GitHub. Ideas, bug reports and pull requests are welcome there.
